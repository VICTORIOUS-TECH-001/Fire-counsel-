## C. Material facts

The respondents (as plaintiffs) had obtained loan and overdraft facilities from the appellant bank, secured by mortgage deeds (Exhibits 25, 26 and 27). They later contended that the bank had agreed to grant them a further or additional facility, and that the bank was not entitled to vary the interest rate without their consent or to exercise its power of sale. The dispute turned on whether a further facility had ever been agreed. Despite protracted correspondence, there was no evidence of the amount of the alleged additional loan, the period of repayment, the rate of interest, or the security to be provided. The bank relied on the written mortgage deeds; the respondents sought to rely on extraneous understandings.

## D. Procedural history

The trial court and the Court of Appeal dealt with the claims and cross-claims; the matter reached the Supreme Court as SC.236/1991 and was determined on 30 September 1994. MARK: the precise orders of the Supreme Court were not established from the reproduction read.

## E. Issues for determination

Analytical summary: (1) whether there was a concluded agreement for a further or additional loan facility; and (2) whether extrinsic evidence was admissible to add to, vary or contradict the written mortgage deeds.

## F. Decision / holding

There was no concluded agreement for a further facility. The amount of the loan, the period of repayment, the rate of interest and the security were fundamental terms which had to be provided for, and in the absence of agreement on fundamental terms there can be no valid contract. Extrinsic evidence was inadmissible to add to, vary, subtract from or contradict the terms of the written mortgage deeds, and it was not the business of a court to make a contract for the parties.

## G. Ratio decidendi

Where the parties to a transaction have not agreed on its fundamental terms, there is no valid contract; the court will not fill the gap. Where they have reduced their agreement to writing, extrinsic evidence is inadmissible to add to, vary, subtract from or contradict the written instrument, and the written document is the authoritative legal source for resolving disagreements about its terms.

## H. The exact legal rule

VERIFIED quotations: 'The foregoing were fundamental terms which should be provided for in such a transaction. In the absence of agreement on the fundamental terms, there can be no valid contract.' And: 'It is not the business of a court to make a contract for the parties or to rewrite the one which they have made.'

## I. Exceptions, limits and qualifications

(1) The parol evidence rule is subject to the recognised exceptions (custom, trade usage, implied terms, rectification, condition precedent, collateral contract, ambiguity), none of which was established on these facts. (2) A term may be implied where it is necessary, but not merely because it would have been convenient: 'Such a term could not be implied merely because it would have made things more convenient.'

## J. Application to the subject

Supplies the second condition on the uploaded page — that a proposition must be definite and certain as to its terms. It is the clearest Nigerian statement that a purported offer which leaves the essential terms open is not an offer capable of acceptance at all, because acceptance of it could not produce a complete contract. It also links back to Tejumola and Adebanjo: all three are cases in which the parties had talked at length but had never agreed the critical term.

## K. Later treatment

Applied routinely: the Court of Appeal in Akudigwe v Attorney-General of Anambra State (2020) quotes Sax for the proposition that parties who say different things at different times are not ad idem and no valid contract is formed. Cited in Shobanke v Sarki (2005) and in the banking-law literature on interest-rate variation. No decision overruling or doubting it was located.

## L. Examination significance

Cite it for: the requirement that fundamental terms be agreed before a contract exists, and for the parol evidence rule in written contracts. Do NOT cite it as authority for the definition of an offer as such — it is authority on certainty and completeness. The commonest mistake is the misprinted part number '(Pt 36)' found in some judgments and notes; the correct part is 361.

## Case prepared for exam — fast writing

**Citation and court.** Union Bank of Nigeria Ltd v Sax (Nigeria) Ltd & 2 Ors (1994) 8 NWLR (Pt 361) 150 (SC).

**Facts.**

1. The respondents had borrowed from the appellant bank on overdraft and loan facilities secured by three mortgage deeds.
2. They later claimed that the bank had agreed to grant them an additional facility and could not vary interest without their consent.
3. The correspondence showed no agreement on the amount of the extra loan, on repayment, on interest or on security.
4. The bank relied on the written mortgage deeds, while the respondents sought to rely on understandings outside those documents.
5. The Supreme Court held that the missing items were fundamental terms and that no valid contract for a further facility existed.
6. It also held that extrinsic evidence could not be used to add to or vary the written deeds, and that courts do not make contracts for parties.

**Issue.** Whether a valid contract for an additional loan existed, and whether extrinsic evidence could vary the written mortgage deeds.

**Held.** No contract for a further facility; extrinsic evidence was inadmissible; the court will not rewrite the parties' bargain.

**Rule / ratio.** There can be no valid contract where the fundamental terms have not been agreed, and where parties have reduced their agreement to writing, extrinsic evidence cannot add to, vary or contradict it.

**Exception or qualification.** Terms may be admitted or implied outside the written document only on recognised grounds such as custom, trade usage, ambiguity, rectification, collateral contract or necessity — not mere convenience.

**Why it matters.** It is the Nigerian Supreme Court's clearest statement that certainty of terms is a precondition of an offer capable of acceptance.

## Mnemonics

- **Case-name cue:** SAX: the bank wanted the notes in writing — 'SAX the record, or there is no contract'.
- **Facts:** A-P-R-I-S: Amount · Period of repayment · Rate of interest · Instrument in writing · Security.
- **Rule:** NO FUNDAMENTAL TERMS → NO CONTRACT, and NO WRITING → NO EXTRINSIC VARIATION.
- **Trigger:** When an alleged agreement leaves the price, amount, duration or subject matter undecided, or when one side tries to add oral terms to a written contract.
- **Recall drill:** List the four fundamental terms that were missing in Sax, and state the parol evidence rule with one exception.
