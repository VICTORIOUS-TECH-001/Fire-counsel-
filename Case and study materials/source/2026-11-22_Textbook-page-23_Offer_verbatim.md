# OFFER

## 2.1. Meaning of Offer

An offer is an expression of willingness to contract on certain terms made with the
intention that it shall become binding as soon as it is accepted by the person to
whom it is addressed.^1^

For easier understanding, an offer is a proposition made by one party called the
offeror to another party called the offeree clearly and precisely indicating the
terms under which the offeror is willing to enter into a contract with the offeree.^2^

In *Dahiru v Kamale*^3^ Ogbuagu, JSC said:

> An 'offer' it is also settled, is an expression of readiness to contract on the terms
> specified by the offeror which if accepted by the offeree, will give rise to a binding
> contract. In other words, it is by acceptance that the offer is converted into a
> contract.^4^

An offer is the first ingredient to look out for in ascertaining whether there is a
contract or not. Where there is no offer, there is no contract.

## 2.2. Conditions for an Offer

In order for a proposition to amount to an offer capable of acceptance, four
conditions must be satisfied:

First, the proposition must be definite, certain, unequivocal and final. In other
words, it must amount to a definite promise by the offeror to be bound, provided
that certain specified terms are accepted. For this reason, a true offer creates the
power of acceptance. This means that when once an offer has been made, the
offeror has completely done his part towards the making of the agreement. The
only thing remaining is the part to be done by the offeree.
